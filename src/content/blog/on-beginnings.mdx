# On Beginnings

The ancient Greeks believed that all things begin in chaos, a formless void from which order slowly emerges. And perhaps they were right.

I remember sitting with my dad in our balcony on a warm summer evening. We were watching the sun set over the city skyline when he turned to me and said:

> "The most dangerous thing you can do is wait for the perfect moment. Beginnings are never perfect; they are simply *necessary*."

I didn't understand him then. I was young, obsessed with planning, with getting everything *just right* before starting. But over the years, I've come to see the wisdom in those words.

## The Courage to Begin

Starting something new - a project, a career, a relationship - requires a peculiar kind of courage. It's not the courage of certainty, but the courage of *uncertainty*. The willingness to step into the unknown and trust that you'll figure it out along the way.

As Lao Tzu wrote:

> "A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step."

But what he didn't say is that the hardest part isn't the thousand miles. It's convincing yourself to take that first step.

## A Note on Imperfection

I've learned to embrace imperfect beginnings. This portfolio, this very blog post, is itself a beginning: rough around the edges, uncertain of its direction. And that's okay.

Because perfect is the enemy of done. And done is the enemy of never starting at all.

*"Begin at the beginning," the King said gravely, "and go on till you come to the end: then stop."*
— Lewis Carroll, Alice in Wonderland
